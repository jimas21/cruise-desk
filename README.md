# Deal Desk

A native iPhone app for the cruise deal desk: the deals Claude finds, one glance per deal, answered with a swipe.

- **Deals**: new deals with no answer yet, in two buckets with counts: **Best deals** (everything, best score
  first) and **For my trip** (the ones that pass My Trip, best value first). A sort menu (best score, lowest total,
  biggest % under typical, soonest sailing, newest) and a filter sheet (ship class, nights, cabin, port or region,
  sail month, most for the trip) work on both. Each deal is a calm summary sheet: ship and class, dates and nights,
  the route with its stops, the cabin (and whether the ocean view is confirmed), the total for 3 with the price per
  person, a price meter (low, typical, high) with how far under typical it is, and why it's worth a look. In Best
  deals, a deal that misses My Trip says why in one small line. Swipe right for Interested, left for Not for me or
  Later. Tap for the details: **The scoop** (Claude's plain-language write-up) first, then price, cabin options,
  the day-by-day itinerary, a price-history sparkline, perks and deadlines, sources, and an **Ask** thread.
- **Any cruise line**: every deal carries its line and a size tier (small, mid-size, large, mega, by tonnage), shown
  above the ship's name. **My Lines** picks the lines Deal Desk watches (or every line), each with what it can
  honestly see today: tracking live, limited data or news only.
- **Tracking**: pick the exact sailing (line, ship, date, nights, cabin, and the exact category if you like) from
  the sailings Deal Desk already knows, from a deal (the binoculars), or by pasting a sailing link (read on the
  phone; nothing is fetched). Each one shows now vs typical vs the lowest seen with a price sparkline; fields read
  "filling…" until the server fills them from its fare data. Swipe left to stop tracking.
- **My Trip**: lines, ship sizes, Royal Caribbean classes, length, cabin, who's going, budget, ports, regions, dates
  and sail months, what counts as a deal (new low, % under typical, price drop, sale, fits my trip; the lowest
  score), alerts and how often to check, in plain words. The buckets re-sort on the phone the moment it is saved;
  Claude reads the same settings on its next check.
- **Ask Deal Desk**: ask about one deal (in its detail) or anything at all (the speech-bubble button on Deals).
  The deal server answers within a minute or two and an "Answer ready" alert opens the thread.
- **Reviewed**: answered deals by answer, with Claude's reply.
- **Save**: every way to save, live offers first with their deadlines, then standing tricks.
- **Rules**: Claude's suggested rules (Use / No), your rules (Pause), paused and declined ones, and what Claude
  looks for.

It reads and writes the `dealdesk-api` server described in the cruise-watch design notes (`prefs/trip` for My Trip,
`questions` for Ask, `GET /v1/deals/{id}/history` for the sparkline, and from v3 `GET /v1/catalog`, `tracked` and
`POST /v1/refresh`): `GET /v1/sync` on launch
and on pull to refresh, cached on the phone so it opens instantly and works offline (answers made offline are sent
when the phone is back online), and versioned writes that re-sync and retry once when something changed at the same
time. Alerts arrive as native push; a tap opens that deal. `dealdesk://deal/<id>` links do the same,
`dealdesk://tracked/<id>` opens a tracked sailing and `dealdesk://track?link=<page>` starts tracking from a page
(a Shortcut can hand one over). On a v3 server, opening the app also asks for a light refresh of the sources that
allow automated checks (at most every 5 minutes) and syncs again 90 seconds later. Until `GET /v1/catalog` says
the server is v3, the app keeps its v2 behaviour: Tracking, My Lines and coverage say "coming soon", and no v3
field or tracked write is ever sent.

## Building

No Mac is involved. Everything runs on GitHub Actions; the Xcode project is generated by XcodeGen from
`project.yml` and never committed.

| Workflow | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Compile check` | here | Unsigned simulator build on every push, then the unit tests (`Tests/`) on an iPhone simulator. It also renders every screen from sample data, light and dark, and uploads them as the run's `screenshots` artifact. No secrets. |
| `TestFlight` | here | Started by the Release workflow on each new `vX.Y.Z` tag (or by hand). Preflight, checks the version matches the tag, registers the bundle ID and turns on Push Notifications for it (`ci/asc_setup.py`), checks the App Store Connect app record, picks the build number, draws the icon (`ci/make_icon.py`), archives with the server address and app token from secrets, and uploads to TestFlight. |
| `Release` | here | release-please on every push to main: a `feat`, `fix` or `perf` commit becomes a release (tag, GitHub release, `CHANGELOG.md`) and starts TestFlight. See `RELEASING.md`. |

Settings the TestFlight workflow needs (names only; values live in GitHub secrets): variable `TEAM_ID`, secrets
`ASC_KEY_ID`, `ASC_ISSUER_ID`, `ASC_KEY_P8`, `DEALDESK_BUNDLE_ID`, `DEALDESK_BASE_URL`, `DEALDESK_APP_TOKEN`.

One-time steps only the account holder can do:

1. Create the App Store Connect app record for the bundle ID (the workflow stops and prints the exact steps if it
   is missing).
2. Create an APNs key (.p8) in the developer portal (Keys > + > Apple Push Notifications service) and give it to
   the deal server, which sends the alerts.

## Artwork

All original, no trademarks and no text. The app icon and the launch-screen mark are drawn at build time by
`ci/make_icon.py` (Pillow): a porthole looking out on a golden-hour sea with a small ship on the horizon. Inside the
app everything is vector SwiftUI (`App/UI/Art.swift`): the sea scene at the top of Deals (a night sky with moon and
stars in dark mode), the kind-tinted hero behind a deal's name, and the calm-sea pictures on empty screens. The waves
drift slowly and stand still under Reduce Motion; the art is hidden from VoiceOver.

## Settings on the phone

The build carries the server address and app token (Info.plist `DDBaseURL` / `DDAppToken`, injected by CI; the
repository holds only placeholders). The token is copied into the Keychain at launch. Settings can override either
one by pasting, for recovery, and test the connection.
